Fuel prices were regulated and identical at every station in the country. The chain needed a reason for drivers to choose its stations, and to keep coming back.

The challenge

With price off the table, the market relied on car giveaways and raffles. They were good for awareness, but had no clear link to how much fuel customers actually bought.

The campaign

We replaced luck with frequency. Every fill-up earned progress toward a reward, so the more often drivers chose the chain, the sooner they got there.

The results

Over 14 weeks, weekly fuel volume rose from 747,760 to 809,089 gallons, an 8.2% increase, and drivers redeemed more than 100,000 rewards.

Why it worked

Rewarding behavior instead of chance ties every dollar of the campaign to the result the retailer wants: more fill-ups, more often.
